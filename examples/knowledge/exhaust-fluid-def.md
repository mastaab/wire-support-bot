# Exhaust fluid (DEF) warnings

Fictional example for testing the document index. It describes an invented truck fleet and is not real vehicle guidance.

The trucks of the example fleet clean their exhaust with diesel exhaust fluid (DEF). The fluid has its own tank with a blue cap next to the fuel tank. Without fluid, or with a fault in the exhaust treatment, the engine reduces its power to protect the environment.

## Low fluid level

### First warning

When the fluid level drops to about one tenth of the tank, a yellow fluid symbol comes on and the display shows the remaining range. Refill at the next opportunity; most truck stops and every depot sell the fluid at a separate pump.

### Final warning and reduced power

If the tank is not refilled, the symbol starts to flash and the display warns that engine power will be reduced. After the tank runs empty the truck may not restart after switching off. Refill before switching off the engine whenever the symbol flashes.

## Refilling

Use only certified exhaust fluid from a sealed pump or container. Never put fuel, water or any other liquid into the fluid tank, and never put fluid into the fuel tank; if this happens, do not start the engine and call the fleet desk. Spilled fluid crystallizes into a white crust: rinse it off with water. After refilling, the warning goes out within a few minutes of driving.

## Exhaust treatment fault

If the warning stays on after a refill, or the display shows an exhaust treatment fault, the fluid may be contaminated or a sensor, heater or pump may have failed. In cold weather the fluid can freeze; the system heats it, so a warning shortly after a cold start can go out by itself after some driving. If the warning stays on for more than a day, or power is already reduced, book a workshop visit or call the fleet desk.

## Good practice

Keep the blue cap closed and clean, do not store fluid containers in the cab in hot weather, and note every refill in the trip log.
