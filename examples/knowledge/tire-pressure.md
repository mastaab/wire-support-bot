# Tire pressure and tire changes

Fictional example for testing the document index. It describes an invented truck fleet and is not real vehicle guidance.

Correct tire pressure keeps the truck stable, saves fuel and makes tires last longer. Most tire damage in the example fleet starts with pressure that was too low for weeks.

## Checking the pressure

### How often

Check the pressure of every tire once a week and before every long trip, always with cold tires (before driving or after at least three hours of rest). Check it also after the tire pressure warning light came on and after driving over a curb or debris.

### How to check

Use the calibrated gauge from the cab kit. Remove the valve cap, press the gauge firmly onto the valve and read the value. Compare it with the pressure on the sticker inside the driver's door: the sticker lists the values for the steer axle, the drive axle and the trailer axles, for empty and loaded trucks. Refit every valve cap.

### Adjusting the pressure

Fill up at a depot or a truck stop with a tire inflator. Never let air out of a hot tire to reach the sticker value: hot tires always read higher. Write the values in the trip log.

## Tire pressure warning light

When the yellow tire symbol comes on, reduce speed, avoid sharp steering and stop at the next safe place. Walk around the truck and look for a flat or visibly low tire. If a tire is clearly flat or damaged, do not drive on: report a breakdown. If all tires look normal, check the pressures and fill up; if the light comes back within a day, book a workshop visit.

## Tire wear and damage

Check the tread depth monthly with the gauge in the cab kit. Replace a tire when the tread is close to the wear markers, or at once when it has cuts, bulges, exposed cords or stones stuck deep in the tread.

## Tire changes

Drivers do not change truck tires themselves. A flat or damaged tire is replaced by the roadside tire service, which the fleet desk sends. Give the desk the truck's fleet number, the location and which tire is affected (axle and side, inner or outer for twin tires). Seasonal tire changes are planned by the depot and booked in the workshop calendar.
