# Breakdowns: frequently asked questions

Fictional example for testing the document index. It describes an invented truck fleet and is not real vehicle guidance.

## What do I do first after a breakdown?

Stop as far from traffic as possible, switch on the hazard lights, put on the high-visibility vest and set up the warning triangle at the distance the road requires. Leave the cab on the side away from traffic and wait behind the barrier if there is one.

## Who do I call?

Call the fleet desk on the number printed on the sticker in the cab. The desk is staffed day and night and sends roadside assistance, a tire service or a recovery truck as needed. In an emergency with injuries, fire or a danger to other road users, call the emergency services first and the fleet desk afterwards.

## What does the fleet desk need from me?

The truck's fleet number, your exact location (road, direction and the nearest marker post or exit, or the location from the navigation display), what happened, which warning lights are on, whether the truck is loaded and with what, and whether the load needs cooling. Photos of the instrument cluster and of any visible damage help the desk decide what to send.

## Can I keep driving with a warning light?

It depends on the color: with a red light stop as soon as it is safe, with a yellow light the truck can usually be driven carefully to the end of the trip. See the dashboard warning lights guide.

## How long will I wait?

The fleet desk tells you the expected arrival time of the service when it has been booked. If nobody has arrived by then, call the desk again.

## What happens to my load?

For a long repair the desk arranges for another truck to take over the load, and for cooled loads it checks that the cooling unit keeps running. Do not unload or leave the truck unattended unless the desk or the emergency services tell you to.

## Do I need to fill in a report?

Yes. After every breakdown, write a short note in the trip log: the time, the place, what happened and who helped. The workshop uses it to find recurring faults.
